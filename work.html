Toxic Language and Social Endorsement: Evidence from YouTube News Comments

2026
BSc dissertation, University of Warwick

Does toxic language win or lose social approval online? I collect 1.6 million top-level comments on 4,912 videos from four major US news channels through the YouTube Data API. Toxicity is scored with Google's Perspective API, political content is classified with a natural-language-inference model, and similar comments are grouped with TF-IDF and cosine-similarity nearest neighbours so that toxic and non-toxic comments are compared within the same discussion.

Using high-dimensional fixed-effects models and augmented inverse-probability weighting (random forests with cross-fitting), I find that toxic comments receive about 19% fewer likes and are 7 percentage points less likely to be liked at all. Toxic content can hold attention while lowering endorsement, so engagement metrics should not be read as a single measure of user preference.

Exploring User Engagement in Political YouTube News: A Study on BBC Breaking News

2024
Undergraduate Research Support Scheme (URSS), University of Warwick

This study explores negativity bias in user engagement with YouTube news. It compares comments and comment likes between political and non-political BBC Breaking News videos, with sentiment scored by a RoBERTa model and relationships estimated with OLS and instrumental variables. Political news attracts more negative comments, while negative comments on political news receive relatively fewer likes.
